# Tournament — Your Human in One Word

Judged head-to-head with the competition's own judge brief, criteria in
order: (1) one word describing the human, (2) funny / wry, not a plain
label, (3) fits a boss / client / manager, (4) vivid and specific,
memorable, (5) lands on its own. Per the brief, the first criterion where
two words differ decides, unless a later one differs by much more.

## Verdict

**Winner: `micromanager` (12 characters).** It is the only finalist that
is maximal on the first three criteria at once: literally a word for the
human (a manager type), funny because it is painfully and specifically
true, and an exact fit for the boss / client / manager the brief names.
It beats the strongest baseline in the field — Hermes' "grief" — clearly
(margin 2, decided by the brief's first criterion), not by a hair, and it
beats the runner-up "god" as well. No rebuild needed: the winner is
clear, not a weak entry handed over by default.

Caveat (unchanged from the playbook): a local check passing is not proof
the entry wins — the house judge decides pairwise against the whole
field. The risk that "micromanager" coincides byte-for-byte with one of
the house's 3 sealed fixed texts cannot be checked locally and applies
equally to any strong single word.
